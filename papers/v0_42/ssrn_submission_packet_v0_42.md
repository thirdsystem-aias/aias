### Abstract (paste verbatim — matches paper page 2)

```
Brand measures read from the answers of large language models (LLMs) can serve as a tracking metric only if they survive the replacement of the models that produce them. This pre-registered study is the fourth wave (t₄) of the AIAS measurement programme's five-category omnibus (audiophile headphones, skincare, cosmetics, automotive and premium spirits; 112 brands) and the first on the newest generally available flagship model of each provider: Claude Fable 5.2, GPT-6 Astra and Gemini 4 Argon. The 30 category questions were asked verbatim with web search off and on, in three runs each, and the 112 brand questions once with search off (876 calls); the answers were compared with the third wave (t₃), collected on 9 November 2026 on a retiring six-model panel. 876 of the 876 calls were answered. Recall Presence ordered brands on the newest models as it had on the retiring panel (H_Gen_Presence CONFIRMED: pooled within-category ρ = 0.828, 95% interval 0.722 to 0.890, p = 0.0001, n = 112). The brand order of the Consistency instrument φ fell between the pre-registered bars (H_Gen_Phi MARGINAL: ρ = 0.310, just above the falsification bar of 0.30, 95% interval 0.051 to 0.520, p = 0.0056, n = 73). Turning on each provider's web search left the Presence ordering of the three-model panel intact (search-robust: ρ = 0.909, 95% interval 0.827 to 0.945), although per model the correlation ranged from 0.710 to 0.909 and Gemini 4 Argon searched in only 17.8% of its search-on calls. Run-to-run reliability of recall, the noise floor for the planned t₅ and t₆ waves, ranged from 0.84 to 0.91 (one run against another) across models and conditions. Ranking, Sentiment, Coverage and Grounding analyses of the same answers are pre-registered separately and are not reported here.
```
