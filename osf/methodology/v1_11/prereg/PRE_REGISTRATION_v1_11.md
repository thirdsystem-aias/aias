## 4. Fixed rules

### 4.1 Unit

- **R (recognition):** the whole answer, one unit per (brand, model, wave); cut at 16,000 characters (flagged). The longest R answer has 10,058 characters; none is cut.
- **F and E (long-form):** one **mention window** per (answer, brand) for every registry brand the answer names.
  - **Which brands an F answer names** is the program's certified mention record, read directly from the sources behind `osf/v41/scripts/score_v41.load_wave` and v1.9's `mentions`: v0.20–v0.22 the phase's certified `detect_mention` (`scripts/score_v20.py`, `score_v21.py`, `score_v22.py`); v0.19 the coded `phase_b_results.csv`; v0.23 the coded `v23_phase_b_scored.json`. So n here equals Presence's mention count for every omnibus unit (checked at Stage C against `n_mentions`). v1.10's extractor (E2) names exactly the same brands in every F answer (pre-lock parity 1.0000 at every wave, §8).
  - **Which brands an E answer names** is v1.10's E2 rule (registry aliases, word-bounded, on v1.4-normalised text) with the registry listed in §2, for all eight sets. The archived `brands_canonical` column (gpt-5.4-mini function calling; present in the five v0.9 files only) builds no unit; its agreement with E2 is reported (§8).
- **Window (brand block):** the answer is split into segments (newline or two or more spaces; inline headings, bullets and numbered items; then sentences). The window is every segment naming the brand, each followed by up to 4 continuation segments, stopping at a heading or list label or at a segment naming another registry brand; gaps are marked `[...]`; capped at 1,500 characters (whole segments; a first segment longer than the cap is cut at it). A segment *names* a brand when a match of one of the brand's registry aliases (case-insensitive, accent-folded, word-bounded) found on the **whole answer** overlaps it. Matching on the whole answer (a pre-lock fix, §15) locates aliases that the sentence splitter cuts — `e.l.f. Cosmetics`, `St. George Spirits`, `Zwilling J.A. Henckels`, `Friedr. Dick`, `R. Twining and Company` — which the draft's per-segment matching could not; the segments themselves are unchanged (tested against the draft code).
- **Extraction miss:** a certified mention with no locatable window is not judged and is counted. If misses exceed 5% of a set's mentions, the run halts before any judge call (U0) and the runner reports. U0 on the frozen inputs: **0 misses** in all 23 F and E sets.
- **Cap per brand:** at most 36 windows per brand × answer set × wave (seed 111001, round-robin over answering models). A no-op for F (at most 36 by design; asserted); it limits cost for brands named in most of an early set's 288 answers.

### 4.2 Judge

- **J1 (primary): Claude Opus 5.5, `claude-opus-5-5`, through Anthropic's Message Batches API. J2 (reliability): GPT-6.1 Sol, `gpt-6.1-sol`, through OpenAI's Batch API (`/v1/responses`). Alternate: Gemini 4 Argon, `gemini-4-argon`, synchronous API.** Neither judge is its provider's flagship: Claude Fable 5.2 is Anthropic's and GPT-6 Astra (the J2 of r1 before the budget redesign) is OpenAI's. The tier choice is the budget redesign of §7 (decision 21): rating the valence of a short text on a five-point scale with explicit anchors does not need a flagship, and whether the chosen tiers suffice is not assumed but tested — by the agreement gate H1 (two judges from different providers must agree) and by Paper B's human labels. J1 and J2 come from different providers. IDs: `claude-opus-5-5` as recorded in the v0.42 pre-registration §4.3 and listed on Anthropic's effort documentation (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort, accessed 2026-11-10); `gpt-6.1-sol` from OpenAI's model page (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6.1-sol, accessed 2026-11-10: "Near-Astra performance for complex work at a lower cost"; Responses, Chat Completions and Batch endpoints; structured outputs supported; `reasoning.effort` low, medium (default), high, xhigh, max); `gemini-4-argon` from Google's pricing page ("Our efficient Gemini 4 frontier reasoning model").
- **Alternate.** The alternate replaces J1 or J2 only if that judge cannot be used: the model is unavailable or retired before its run ends, or its served model ID fails the check below on its calibration calls (§7). The replacement is recorded (who, when, why) before any further call and before any output is scored; the replacing model judges all of the replaced judge's units; the replaced judge's outputs are kept and reported, never scored.
- **Call parameters (fixed; `judge_prompt_v1_11.md`).** No sampling parameter is sent to any judge (no temperature, top_p or top_k). Reasoning runs at the lowest effort each vendor documents for the model, fixed for the run: Claude Opus 5.5 `output_config.effort = "low"` — Anthropic's effort page (accessed 2026-11-10) lists the levels max, xhigh, high, medium, low, with `low` "most efficient", says Opus 5.5 "supports all five effort levels, and `medium` is the default", and that "adaptive thinking is always on and can't be turned off" (`thinking: {"type": "disabled"}` returns a 400 error at every effort level); GPT-6.1 Sol `reasoning.effort = "low"` (the lowest value on its model page); Gemini 4 Argon `thinking_config.thinking_level = "low"` (temperature ignored, not sent). **Batch:** Anthropic's batch page (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/batch-processing, accessed 2026-11-10) states that "almost any request you can make to the Messages API can be included in a batch", lists extended thinking as supported and excludes only `stream`, `speed` (fast mode), `fallbacks` and `max_tokens: 0` — so `output_config.effort` and a system prompt are accepted in a batch; OpenAI's batch guide lists `/v1/responses` and says each line's body takes "the same parameters" as the endpoint. The request body is byte-for-byte the same in batch and synchronous mode. **Maximum output 4,096 tokens**, reasoning included (an output cap; was 16,384): the longest valid reply is 53 tokens (o200k count of a reply with a 20-word quote, `token_counts_v1_11.json`) and the largest reasoning count in the program's probes is 375 tokens (v0.42 cost inputs), so 4,096 leaves more than ten times the planning output while bounding each call's worst case; a reply cut by the cap is invalid output and is retried as in this section. No tools, no web search. Prompt-only JSON for every judge (no response schema is sent), with the identical prompt text. Every request parameter is written to the record of every call (`request`), with the served model ID, finish reason, input, output and reasoning tokens, cost, timestamps, the endpoint and, for batch calls, the batch ID; the interpreter and SDK versions are recorded with the run (the system Python 3.14 with anthropic 0.102.0, openai 2.37.0 and google-genai 2.3.0 on 2026-11-10; checked offline on 2026-11-10 that anthropic's `messages.batches.create/retrieve/results` take `custom_id` + `params` and its message parameters include `output_config`, and that openai's `batches.create` accepts `endpoint="/v1/responses"` with `completion_window="24h"` and its Responses parameters include `reasoning`, `max_output_tokens` and `instructions`).
- **Served model.** The served ID must equal the requested ID or a dated snapshot of it; otherwise the response counts as invalid (MC_Served, as v0.42).
- **Blinding:** the judge sees brand, category, the question, the text type and the text; never the answering model, the wave, the answer set, or any P, C* or Ranking value.
- **Call order:** phase A = R and F (J1 and J2 in parallel); A2 = their self-consistency repeats; B = E; B2 = E repeats. Within a phase and judge the order is shuffled (seed 111000).
- **Scale:** valence −2…+2 with the anchors in the prompt, plus `refers_to_brand`, `evaluative`, `mixed` and a ≤ 20-word evidence quote.
- **Invalid output** (not the schema, inconsistent fields, empty, or served-ID mismatch): up to 2 retries with identical input; then JUDGE_FAIL (excluded, counted). JUDGE_FAIL above 2% of any set's units (per judge) halts before scoring. An API error that persists through the transient-retry policy (3 attempts) is re-sent once; a unit never answered is UNJUDGED (counted, excluded).
- **Coverage:** J1 judges every judged unit (7,578). J2 judges every R and F unit and a pre-registered, seeded 10% stratified random sample of E windows (by category × answering model; seed 111002, drawn at U0): 3,650 units. Justification by precision (`precision_v1_11.json`, synthetic valences only, built to sit at α ≈ 0.68): H1's brand ρ is computed on F units only, which J2 judges in full (163 substrate × wave × brand cells have ≥ 6 F windows; Fisher-z 95% interval at ρ = 0.80: 0.74–0.85), so it does not depend on the E share; H1's α uses about 3,649 unit pairs (R + F + the E sample), 95% bootstrap half-width ±0.017, against ±0.011 if J2 judged every E window (7,578 pairs; US$12.88 more at r = 270) — both far inside the 0.167 between the α thresholds (0.667, 0.50); the E block's own α (436 pairs) is descriptive (§6.5), ±0.040.
- **Self-consistency (replaces draft r0's 2% determinism check, which assumed temperature 0).** A fixed 5% sample of the judged units, drawn within block at U0 (seed 111003; 379 units: R 22, F 139, E 218), is judged a second time by each judge on the units it judges (J1 379, J2 184), with identical input, after that judge's main pass of the block. Reported per judge and block: exact valence agreement, ordinal α and κ on `evaluative`; below 0.90 exact agreement is flagged in Paper A (not a halt). The first judgement is the one scored.

### 4.3 Near-neutral text

Being named, listed or recommended by inclusion is not evaluation; plain facts are neutral; reported third-party judgements (awards, ratings, recalls, controversies) count as evaluation (prompt rules 2–4). Non-evaluative units are scored **0 (= 50 on the 0–100 unit scale) and kept in the score** — a neutral description is an outcome, not missing data; dropping it would make S rest on the few evaluative units. Units with `refers_to_brand = false` are excluded and counted. Two descriptives travel with S but are not the score: E = evaluative share; NV = mean unit score among evaluative units (reported only with ≥ 3 evaluative units).

### 4.4 Score and n floor

Unit score u = 25·(valence + 2) ∈ {0, 25, 50, 75, 100}. **S(brand, set, wave) = mean u over the brand's valid units**, each unit weight 1; scored on J1's outputs (J2 is for reliability only). **S is rated only if n ≥ 6 valid units; otherwise NOT RATED** (no shrinkage). Justification: (i) mirrors v1.9's C* rule, and because F windows are counted exactly as Presence's mentions, S and C* are rated on the same omnibus units — like is compared with like; (ii) six units is one model's full frame set; (iii) v1.9 showed that shrinking toward a midpoint by mention count ties a component to Presence (SHRINK failed K1), so the floor, not shrinkage, is used. For R, n = 6 means all six models described the brand in that wave.

The **AIAS Sentiment score is the long-form S** (F for the omnibus, E for the early categories). The recognition score S_rec (block R) is a second elicitation for one category, used for a floor-free separability check, a convergence check and Paper B's sample.

## 5. Hypotheses and decision rules

**Conventions.** ρ_w = pooled within-category Spearman: average ranks are computed within each substrate (category) and **centred on its mean rank, r − (m_c + 1)/2**; ρ_w is the Pearson correlation of the pooled centred ranks (v1.10 §7; computed with v1.10's own functions). The uncentred v0.37–v0.41 convention is reported as sensitivity SU (§5.1). Units = brand × wave (F, R) or brand × set (E). A substrate or category enters a pooled statistic only with **≥ 4 units** in that comparison; a statistic is **UNDETERMINED** with fewer than **20 units** pooled or fewer than **3 substrates / categories** entering (H8, one substrate by design: ≥ 20 units). Band verdicts use the program bands: **|ρ| ≤ 0.30 CONFIRMED separable; |ρ| ≥ 0.50 FALSIFIED; between MARGINAL.** **Three-wave rule** for omnibus separability — v1.10's locked rule (`verdict_separability` at tag `v1.10-prereg-r2`; decision 22), applied in the same order: **UNDETERMINED if fewer than 2 of t1, t2, t3 are determined; otherwise FALSIFIED iff |ρ| ≥ 0.50 at any determined wave; CONFIRMED iff |ρ| ≤ 0.30 at every determined wave; MARGINAL otherwise.** (A wave is determined by this section's floors, not v1.10's 30-unit floor: the two papers' units differ.) A 95% bootstrap interval (units resampled within substrate, 10,000 draws, seed 111020) is reported with every ρ; verdicts use the point estimate, compared unrounded. Permutation p = (hits + 1)/(N + 1).

**Noise guard.** A noisy instrument is separable from everything. If H1 or H4 is FALSIFIED, H2, H3, H5, H7 and H8 are reported as UNDETERMINED (statistics shown as descriptive only).

Why this design. Batch pricing halves every row, but the same flagship-level pair still projects US$116.79. One judge per provider is kept (H1 needs two providers), at the tier below each provider's flagship: rating the valence of a mention window or a recognition answer on a five-point anchored scale is a classification task, and the decision whether that tier is adequate is left to the data — H1 (two providers must agree: α ≥ 0.667 and brand ρ ≥ 0.80) and Paper B's human labels, which v1.11 waits for before calling Sentiment anything but provisional. J1, which produces every score, stays Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic's second tier, already r1's J1): at batch price it costs US$46.94 at r = 270 before overhead, and replacing it with Sonnet 5.5 would save US$27.00 (with overhead) at the price of a weaker primary scorer, with no budget need. J2's coverage stays R, F and the seeded 10% E sample (§4.2: the precision of α and of brand ρ does not need more; full E coverage would add US$12.88 and lift the r = 375 projection over the pause). **Fit:** the chosen design projects US$66.55 with overhead at the planning value, stays under the US$80 pause while mean reasoning is ≤ 388 tokens per call and under the US$100 cap while it is ≤ 565; input alone is US$21.33. The H1 gate's own units (R and F, never reduced) cost US$43.53 even at r = 375, so the pre-registered tests stay valid under US$100 in every scenario above; no larger budget is requested.

**Hard cap: US$100 of recorded spend, and 30,000 calls, in total for v1.11** (J1 + J2 + retries + repeats + any alternate), enforced before every call and every batch: `judge_v1_11.py` adds the worst case (input tokens bounded by the request's UTF-8 bytes; output at the 4,096-token cap, twice for Gemini; batch price for batch calls) to the recorded spend plus the worst cases in flight, and does not send if the sum would pass US$100; a batch (at most 500 requests; worst case at most US$34.18 for J1 and US$17.07 for J2) is cut to the requests that fit, and waits while other batches hold the room. **At US$80 of recorded spend the run pauses and Pablo is asked**; it resumes only with his recorded approval (`--approve-past-pause "<who, when>"`, logged); batches already in flight are collected. If he does not approve, the run ends there and the analysis uses the units judged (phases run in priority order: R and F first). Spend = the tokens each response reports, at list price (batch price for batch calls; cache discounts not counted); a synchronous call that times out counts at its worst case; a batch item returned without a response is recorded at US$0, as a synchronous API error is; the vendors' invoices are reconciled with the recorded spend after the run and any difference is reported. Every submitted batch is logged before its results are read; an interrupted run re-attaches to its batches (their worst cases held) and never resubmits them.

**Binding projection and reductions.** The binding projection is made once, after the first 200 calls of each judge (calibration, phase A; one batch per judge), from the measured input tokens per request character and output tokens per call (reasoning included), before any E call. If the projected total with 15% overhead passes **US$80** (the pause, so that the cap is never the binding constraint; or 30,000 calls), these reductions apply in this fixed order until it fits: (1) the E window cap 36 → 24; (2) J2's E sample 10% → 5%; (3) E judged on a seeded 50% of each set's answers (seed 111004); (4) E not judged (H7, the early H5 row and the early baseline then rest on the Paper B units only, below). R and F are never reduced, and the Paper B human-label units (§9) are always judged by J1. The decision depends only on token counts, never on any valence, and is written to the plan file (hashed with the frozen outputs) before the run continues; it is applied identically by the scorer (`effective_units`). At r = 270 and at r = 375 no reduction applies. If the alternate replaces a judge, the plan is recomputed from the alternate's own calibration calls (§4.2).

## 11. Limitations

AI-judged and provisional until Paper B. The judges reason at their lowest available effort and no sampling parameter can be fixed, so a judge's output varies from call to call; the self-consistency check measures this, and the reasoning-token budget is an estimate until calibration. The F rated set (n ≥ 6) restricts the Presence range, which can understate |ρ(S, P)|; H8 (floor-free) and H7 (early panel) are the checks. One judge pair; self-preference is tested (H9), not removed — every judge provider also supplied answering models. Two answering panels that are not the newest models; early sets have one wave each. S measures description in answers, not consumer sentiment; no behavioural validity is claimed. The window rule can miss evaluations far from the brand name and can carry a neighbouring sentence; Paper B's labels test the unit as well as the judge. Five of the eight early sets carry archived per-brand labels from a 2026 extraction model (§1a); they were never analysed and enter only an exploratory descriptive. Budget (§7): neither judge is its provider's flagship, J2 judges only 10% of the early windows, and planning token counts are estimates (o200k with vendor factors; reasoning tokens unmeasured until calibration); H1 and Paper B are the checks on the tiers, and the binding projection and the US$100 guard on the estimates.
