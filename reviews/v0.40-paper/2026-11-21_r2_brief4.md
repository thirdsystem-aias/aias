But the paper says what it measured, not what marketing theory learns from it. Since H_Amplify is now conceded as uninformative, the confirmed results are the expected ones: naming follows share, and status answers say dead brands are gone.
- **Length and format:** about 15 pages of main text plus a 4-page appendix. The abstract is about 330 words. The text still carries programme apparatus: the ™ marks, the "Paper status" block, the $ cap, file paths in every figure source line, value keys in Appendix A, commit hashes and S-codes.

**2. The three hardest reviewer objections**

1. **The paper's central theoretical test told us nothing (§3.4, §4).**
   - A Double-Jeopardy-style amplification test that could not reach its threshold leaves the paper with an ordinal share correlation and a status-flag result. A reviewer will call both unsurprising.
   - Table 4 also shows wide model spread with no comment: C1's airline ratio is 0.000 with ρ −0.231, while C2's bank ratio is 1.254.
   - **The paper's current answer:** it concedes the point openly, computes the ceiling and proposes a measure built per answer. It offers no positive claim in its place.
2. **The ghost measures may not be valid (§3.2, §3.5, §3.7).**
   - On recommendation answers the coder's κ is 0.487.
   - The audit is AI-only, and one of its coders, Claude Fable 5.2, is also a tested model.
   - The status question ("can one still book with…") invites a "gone" answer.
   - Living-brand controls were flagged 16.9% of the time overall and 34.9% for the current models.
   - **The paper's current answer:** full disclosure (Limitations 3, 4 and 7), and §3.5 says the rates are "more likely too high than too low". Nothing shows that H_Flag measures more than the wording of the question.
3. **The share inference is fragile and has no rival explanation (§3.3, §2.3, Limitations 6 and 8).**
   - Airlines have only 10 units, and moving Sun Country flips the result (ρ 0.483).
   - The ambiguity rule drops the bare names of the largest airlines: American is named in 216 answers under the rule, 349 counting the bare name.
   - The tests treat pairs as independent, and nothing separates market share from web salience or brand age.
   - The Discover-to-Capital One mapping was not applied as registered (E2).
   - **The paper's current answer:** the sensitivities are reported. Nothing addresses rival predictors or clustering.
