## E1: does the H_Amplify ratio have a ceiling?

**How the scorer counts** (`score_v0_40.py:328-335, 366-374`): k counts each answer that names the leader once. n adds up, unit by unit, the answers that name each unit. There are 432 search-off recommendation answers per category: 16 probes × 9 models × 3 runs. The values file confirms 288 legacy + 144 current for banks.

**My arithmetic:**
- **There is no fixed ceiling in the design.** Since k ≤ 432 and n ≥ k, the ratio is capped only at 1/p₀: 4.63 for airlines and 5.52 for banks. If the claim means a fixed design ceiling, it does not hold.
- **There is a ceiling at the breadth the models actually showed.** Airline answers named 1,668/432 = 3.86 units on average; bank answers named 2,458/432 = 5.69. A leader named in every answer can reach at most k/n = 1/m, where m is that average. So the ratio's ceiling is 1/(m·p₀):
  - airlines: 1/(3.86 × 0.2161) = **1.20**, below 1.25
  - banks: 1/(5.69 × 0.1812) = **0.97**, below parity
- B4's version holds the other units' namings fixed (1,452 and 2,063) and puts the leader in every answer. That gives 432/1,884 ÷ 0.2161 = **1.06** for airlines and 432/2,495 ÷ 0.1812 = **0.955** for banks. Same conclusion.
- **What "flattened" sits beside:** Chase was in 395 of 432 bank answers (91.4%). American was in 216 of 432 (50.0%) only because the bare name "American" is coded ambiguous. With it included (S-Ambig), American was in 349 of 432 (80.8%), and at that breadth (5.56 units per answer) the airline ceiling is 0.83.
- **The binomial null is also mis-specified.** It treats every naming as an independent draw at p₀, but k cannot exceed the number of answers.

**Ruling: holds in substance, not as worded.** It is a ceiling set by the observed breadth of answers, not by the design. It is a blocker for the framing; FALSIFIED stands as registered.

**What the paper must say** (in §3.4, the Fig. 2 caption, the abstract and §4):
- n counts unit-namings, and answers named on average 3.9 airline and 5.7 bank units.
- At that breadth, a leader named in every answer could have reached at most about 1.20 (airlines) and 0.97 (banks). So the amplified threshold was out of reach, and banks could not pass parity.
- Chase appeared in 91% of bank answers, and American in 50% (81% counting the bare name).
- "Flattened" therefore means the leader's share of namings inside answers that list many brands. It does not mean the leader was left out.
- Drop "did not concentrate on the leader", "spreading their naming beyond the leader" and "Two findings are clear" for this half.
- **For Pablo:** the measure's dependence on answer breadth is a property of the locked design.
